According to [this](https://youtu.be/4HM_47UGmk0?si=qSEPWz5UoeRCLb0R), the Exodus as described in the Bible is fiction; it never happened.

At about the 25:09 mark, Korean Egyptologist 곽민수 (한국이집트학연구소/애굽민수) explains that for about 200 years, many scholars tried to find evidence for the Exodus in Egypt, but none has been found.

Due to the lack of concrete evidence for the Exodus, he tenatively holds that it is not historical fact. 

This surprised me; I thought at least _some_ evidence had been found to prove that the Exodus did in fact happen. If the descendants of Jacob lived in Egypt for hundreds of years, it's difficult to believe that there would be no evidence for their extended stay during that time, and their subsequent exodus en masse.

At 27:45, the two scholars discuss the Western intellectual tradition of trying to reconcile religious faith (Catholicism) and rational, scientific inquiry. Just prior to this point in the conversation, 박현도 (서강대 유로메나연구소) - Korean researcher of the Middle East and Islam, discusses how Christmas is not the actual birthday of Jesus Christ. It just happened that Christmas was to celebrate the birthday of the sun god that the Romans worshipped, and once the Romans converted to Christianity, the sun god was replaced with Jesus. And it was the effort of Christian historians who discovered this. 

곽민수 responds to this saying this is due to Western positivism, arguing as follows:
Christianity was the dominant ideology leading up to the 18th century, when the scientific revolution happened and began to have a stronger hold. And the Christians wanted to reconcile their religious faith with science. So they tried to use the tools of scientific inquiry and thought to prove the Bible as historical fact. In that process, many academic fields flourish, one of which is Egyptology. Flinders Petrie, "the father of Egyptology", was initially a biblical archaeologist. In his lifetime efforts to prove the Bible, he did so much to develop the burgeoning field of Egyptology.

A quick search on Perplexity Pro states the following:

> Based on the search results, there is no *definitive* (my emphasis) archaeological evidence that conclusively proves the biblical Exodus account as historically accurate. However, there are some archaeological findings and historical analyses that provide potential support for elements of the Exodus narrative:
> 
>1. Lack of direct evidence: Many archaeologists and scholars note that there is no direct archaeological evidence for the Exodus as described in the Bible, particularly for a large-scale migration of millions of Israelites out of Egypt[1][3].
>
>2. Challenges in finding evidence: Several factors make finding evidence difficult, including:
   - The Israelites were initially slaves and then nomads, leaving little archaeological trace[7].
   - Egyptian records were biased and pharaohs often erased negative events from history[7].
   - Only a small portion of ancient Egypt has been excavated, especially in the Nile Delta region[7].

3. Potential supporting evidence:
   - The Merneptah Stele (c. 1208 BCE) mentions Israel as an established people in Canaan[5].
   - Proto-Sinaitic inscriptions show Semitic writing in Egypt and Sinai during the proposed Exodus period[5].
   - Evidence of Semitic peoples in Egypt during the relevant time periods[7].
   - Similarities between the biblical Tabernacle and Pharaoh Rameses II's battle tent[8].

4. Alternative interpretations:
   - Some scholars suggest the Exodus may have involved a smaller group of people rather than millions[8].
   - The event may have occurred earlier than traditionally thought, possibly during the reign of Akhenaten (c. 1330 BCE)[6].

5. Debate among scholars:
   - While some archaeologists argue there is no evidence for the Exodus, others contend that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence[8].
   - Some scholars believe there is enough contextual evidence to support a historical basis for the Exodus, even if not exactly as described in the Bible[4][8].

In conclusion, while there is no definitive archaeological proof of the biblical Exodus, there are some findings that provide potential support for elements of the narrative. The topic remains a subject of ongoing research and debate among scholars.
